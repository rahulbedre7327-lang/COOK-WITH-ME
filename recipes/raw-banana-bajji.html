📋 Ingredients

  • 2 medium banana
  • 1 cup gram flour
  • 2 tbsp rice flour
  • 1 tsp red chili powder
  • 1/4 tsp turmeric
  • pinch of asafoetida (hing)
  • pinch of baking soda
  • 1/2 tsp carom seeds (ajwain)
  • Water to form a thick batter (~1/2 cup)
  • oil for deep frying;
  • salt to taste

🍳 Required Utensils

  • Mandoline slicer or sharp knife
  • Deep frying pan (Kadai)
  • Mixing bowl & whisk
  • Slotted spoon (jhara)
  • Paper towels for draining

📖 Introduction

Raw Banana Bajji (also known as Vazhakkai Bajji, Balekai Bajji, or Aratikaya Bajji) is a staple South Indian street food snack. Thinly sliced raw plantains are dipped in a spiced gram flour (besan) batter and deep-fried until puffed, golden, and crispy.


🔬 Principle

The technique relies on flash-steaming starchy vegetables inside a sealed, crisp coating. The dense starch structure of the raw plantain softens quickly under internal steam pressure created during deep-frying, while the outer gram flour batter dehydrates into a light, airy shell.


👨‍🍳 Procedure

  1. Peel the raw bananas lightly and slice them thinly lengthwise using a mandoline slicer.
  2. Submerge the slices in a bowl of salted water to prevent them from browning.
  3. In a bowl, combine gram flour, rice flour, carom seeds, red chili powder, turmeric, asafoetida, baking soda, and salt.
  4. Add water gradually while whisking to create a smooth, medium-thick batter that coats the back of a spoon.
  5. Heat oil in a deep pan over medium-high heat until hot.
  6. Pat the banana slices dry, then dip each slice into the batter to coat evenly on both sides.
  7. Gently slide the coated banana slices into the hot oil in batches.
  8. Fry for 3–4 minutes, flipping halfway through until puffed and golden brown.
  9. Drain Excess Oil:Remove with a slotted spoon and drain on paper towels.
  10. Serve hot with coconut chutney or spicy tomato chutney.

⚠ Precautions

  • Ensure Oil Heat is Medium-High: Frying in cold oil causes the raw plantain to absorb excess fat and become greasy.
  • Bloating Risk: Gram flour and raw plantains can cause gas; always include asafoetida (hing) or carom seeds (ajwain) in the batter to ease digestion.

💡 Chef Tips

  • Soak Sliced Plantains: Keep sliced raw bananas submerged in salted water until frying to prevent them from turning black due to oxidation.
  • Thin, Uniform Slices: Use a mandoline slicer; thick slices won't cook through properly in the center before the batter turns dark.
  • Batter Consistency: The batter should be thick enough to coat the slice completely without dripping off too quickly (medium-thick coat).

🍽 Serving Suggestions

  • Serve hot sprinkled with chaat masala alongside coconut chutney or spicy onion-tomato chutney.
  • Pair with South Indian filter coffee or hot ginger tea for a classic rainy-day snack.

🌿 Health Benefits

  • Resistant Starch: Raw plantains are rich in resistant starch and prebiotic fiber, supporting gut health and digestion.
  • Potassium-Rich: Delivers essential potassium and Vitamin B6 for heart health and nerve function.

✅ Result

Texture: A crisp, golden outer batter with a soft, warm, melt-in-the-mouth plantain slice inside. Flavor Profile: Mildly sweet and earthy interior balanced by a savory, spicy, and fragrant coating.
