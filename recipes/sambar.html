Sambar

⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ 4.8 Rating | ⏱ 35 Minutes | 🍽 4 Servings

Category

Lunch/Dinner

Difficulty

Easy

Calories

310 kcal

Cooking Style

South Indian

📋 Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup Toor dal (Pigeon peas)
  • 1/2 tsp Turmeric powder
  • 1/2 tsp Ghee
  • 1.5 cups Mixed vegetables (drumstick, carrot, pumpkin, brinjal,small shallots)
  • 1 medium Tomato (chopped)
  • 1 tbsp Tamarind paste/extract
  • 2 tbsp Sambar powder
  • 1 tbsp Oil or Ghee
  • 1 tsp Mustard seeds
  • 1/4 tsp Fenugreek seeds (methi)
  • 2 Dry red chillies
  • 1 sprig Curry leaves
  • Pinch of Hing (asafoetida)
  • 2 tbsp Fresh coriander leaves (chopped)
  • Salt to taste

🍳 Required Utensils

  • Pressure cooker
  • deep pot/kadai
  • small tadka pan
  • potato masher
  • ladle

📖 Introduction

Sambar is a quintessential South Indian lentil and vegetable stew, infused with tangy tamarind extract, aromatic spices, and finished with a crackling spice tempering.


🔬 Principle

The dish relies on binding three key flavor bases: soft, fully mashed toor dal (pigeon peas) for body, boiled vegetables infused with acid (tamarind), and roasted spices (coriander, cumin, fenugreek, red chillies) bloomed in hot ghee or oil during tempering to release fat-soluble aromatics.


👨‍🍳 Procedure

  1. Cook and Mash the Lentils
  2. Rinse toor dal until water runs clear.
  3. Pressure cook with 1.5 cups water, turmeric, and 1/2 tsp ghee for 4–5 whistles until completely soft.
  4. Mash into a smooth, creamy paste using a whisk or potato masher.
  5. Boil Vegetables with Tamarind
  6. In a deep pot, add the mixed vegetables, chopped tomatoes, tamarind extract, sambar powder, salt, and 2 cups of water.
  7. Simmer over medium heat for 10–12 minutes until the vegetables are tender yet hold their shape.
  8. Combine Lentils and Simmer
  9. Pour the mashed toor dal into the vegetable-tamarind pot.
  10. Adjust consistency with warm water if too thick.
  11. Bring to a gentle boil, then simmer on low heat for 5 minutes to let the flavors meld.
  12. Perform the Tempering (Tadka)
  13. Heat 1 tbsp oil/ghee in a small pan.
  14. Add mustard seeds and let them splutter.
  15. Add fenugreek seeds, dry red chillies, curry leaves, and hing.
  16. Sauté for 10 seconds until fragrant, then pour immediately into the simmering sambar.
  17. Cover with a lid for 2 minutes to lock in aromas.

⚠ Precautions

  • High lentil content may cause gas or acidity in sensitive digestions—do not skip the asafoetida (hing) during tempering.

💡 Chef Tips

  • Always mash the toor dal completely smooth before adding it to the vegetables—unmashed dal grains will settle at the bottom and separate from the water.
  • Boil hard vegetables (like drumsticks and carrots) in the tamarind water first; the acid helps them hold their texture without turning to mush.
  • Add fenugreek seeds (methi) right as the mustard seeds finish popping. Over-browning fenugreek will make the entire sambar bitter.
  • Sambar tastes significantly better 30 minutes after cooking as the lentils absorb the spices and tamarind.

🍽 Serving Suggestions

  • Serve piping hot over steamed rice (Sambar Rice) with a generous drizzle of ghee and a side of potato roast (alu fry) or papadums.
  • Serves as an essential dipping accompaniment for South Indian breakfast staples like Idli, Dosa, Medu Vada, and Uttapam.

🌿 Health Benefits

  • Excellent source of plant-based protein, dietary fiber, and vitamins from diverse vegetables.
  • Spices like turmeric, cumin, and hing aid digestion and reduce bloating.

✅ Result

A hearty, medium-thick stew boasting a complex taste profile—tangy, savory, subtly sweet, and aromatic—studded with tender vegetables and crackling mustard seeds.
